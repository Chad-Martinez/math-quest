# Educational requirements

Math Quest teaches early elementary reasoning with explicit instruction and optional support. The [evidence register](educational-research.md) records the sources and their limits. The [curriculum](curriculum.md) describes the wider game. The [Chapter 1 review](chapter-1.md) identifies its five active math relationships.

## Instruction and mathematics

- Present one primary task, with short concrete instructions and the relevant story, quantities and intermediate results visible. Use accommodations for different attention and movement needs, including inattentive, hyperactive-impulsive and combined ADHD presentations. Offer those supports by need. Never infer a diagnosis or mastery from game behavior.
- Connect objects, representations and symbols. Teach combine, change and compare structures and varying unknown roles. Use explicit demonstration, guided practice, independent work, fresh transfer and later retrieval where the approved activity calls for them. Guide by the relationship and the unknown. Bring in more explicit hints or a worked example as needed.
- Generate validated whole-number variants in the approved range, currently primarily 0–20. Use one mathematical model for the story, answer validation, intermediate results, objects, equations, hints, corrective feedback and teaching guides. Check boundary cases, intermediate values, ties and the least amount needed to exceed a quantity. Use fixed seeds in tests. Render manipulatives from the model.
- Offer direct manipulation with tap and keyboard alternatives to drag, and labels or patterns alongside color. Keep controls visible and focus predictable. Target at least 44 CSS pixels for primary touch targets, check WCAG AA contrast and zoom, and support reduced motion and optional narration. No microphone. Use deliberate reveal for worked answers.
- Keep independent, assisted and worked-example records distinct. Use specific and constructive feedback and celebrate effort and mathematical progress. Leave reasoning untimed and avoid streak pressure, punitive feedback or rewards for speed.

## Parent and movement support

Provide contextual teaching tips for each active problem or template. Include the objective, a guiding question, a concrete demonstration, progressive hints, likely misconceptions and responses, a deliberately revealed worked solution, and a fresh transfer problem with its own answer. Derive all quantities and solutions from their corresponding questions. See [Parent Coach](parent-coach.md) for legacy guide coverage and local evidence behavior.

Keep the scheduled break after Chapter 1’s third stop optional and parent-disableable. Keep the on-demand break available where specified. Allow skipping, flexible pacing and a seated alternative. Avoid enforced timers, cameras and exercise verification. Preserve the response and workspace. Keep movement separate from math outcomes. The duration in an approved kit is a design choice.
