# Curriculum

Math Quest practices relationships among whole numbers through 20. Story Forest joins and separates groups. Racing Valley compares quantities and distinguishes tying from taking the lead. Dinosaur Island asks for an intermediate total before a final calculation. Animal Kingdom and Sports Arena support counting, category selection, numerical comparison, and selecting true statements. Equation Space treats equality as matching values and varies the missing element. Treasure Island rotates across the other skills.

Vocabulary appears in story context and hints. Encourage learners to identify the quantities, the relationship, and what the question asks. Read-aloud, drawing, and hints are optional supports. Worked examples help a learner continue and do not award a solve star.


## Structured teaching

Story Forest now distinguishes combine stories with an unknown whole or part from join and separate stories with an unknown start, change, or result. Racing Valley asks for a difference, the smaller or larger group, a tie, or the smallest addition that gives a lead. The question record labels the relationship schema and unknown role so teaching cues describe what each amount represents. Prompts ask children to identify quantities and the relationship before choosing an operation.

**Learn a skill** offers optional three-item lessons across Story Forest, Racing Valley, and Equation Space. See a worked model, try another item with a visible cue, then try a new item with optional tools. Each stage uses the same structure and a different problem and answer. A completed lesson queues a fourth example for review in a later adventure in that world. The oldest queued skill takes the first normal challenge slot and leaves the configured adventure length intact. Repeating a lesson updates the pending review for that skill. Leaving an unanswered review keeps it queued.

Correct responses in lessons earn stars. Worked examples award no star, and finishing a lesson does not add to adventure completion counts. Guided successes count as assisted. Independent and review items use the usual assistance rules. These outcomes describe the help used in the game.

## Part–part–whole and equality

In **Find a missing part**, move the known number of beads into the red group. Count the remaining beads as the other part. The model always preserves the given whole. Tap or drag individual beads into the bowl.

In Equation Space, the balance compares both sides. Take away from the left for a missing subtraction amount or add to the right for a missing addend. Tap or drag individual blocks and count what you moved when both sides match. Fixed groups on the right represent the separate equation terms. In the worked lesson, **Show the math** links the level scale to the full equation. **Balance by taking away** is also available from **Learn a skill**. These models support the reasoning process while the learner enters and checks their own answer.
