# Educational research and evidence register
Version 1.0 — 25 November 2026. Evidence informs design; it does not validate Math Quest itself.

From the Math Quest Educational and Product Development Handoff v1.0. The original verification labels are preserved. See [educational requirements](educational-requirements.md) for the operational standard.

## R1: Elementary mathematics intervention — verified source summary
What Works Clearinghouse (2021), [Assisting Students Struggling with Mathematics: Intervention in the Elementary Grades](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/26). The guide rates systematic teaching, mathematical language, concrete and visual models, number lines, deliberate word-problem teaching, and timed fluency activities as strongly supported. Apply the first five to reasoning sessions; the decision to defer timed fluency is a product choice, not a claim that timed practice is universally ineffective. The guide concerns elementary intervention broadly, not specifically ADHD or digital games.

## R2: Word problem structures — verified source summary
WWC (2009), [Assisting Students Struggling with Mathematics: Response to Intervention](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/2). Supports explicit instruction and teaching shared underlying word-problem structures. Implement combine/change/compare and varying unknown positions rather than keyword rules.

## R3–R7: References from prior research, pending independent re-verification
- R3 [CDC ADHD in the classroom](https://www.cdc.gov/adhd/treatment/classroom.html): classroom accommodations, clear instructions, feedback and movement breaks.
- R4 [CHADD instructional process](https://chadd.org/for-educators/instructional-process/) and [math assignments](https://chadd.org/for-educators/math-assignments/): practical implementation guidance, not equivalent to randomized evidence.
- R5 Tosto et al. (2015), [ADHD and mathematical ability systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26315934/): previous review reported stronger associations with inattentive symptoms. Association is not causality or individual prediction.
- R6 [Visualization and mathematical word problems in children with ADHD](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-8578.12466): prior discussion described a small study of older children; drawing own models is promising, not proven for first graders. Verify sample, outcomes and bibliographic details before quoting.
- R7 [School-based ADHD intervention meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12318974/): prior discussion reported academic and inattention benefits with less clear hyperactivity effects. Verify exact estimates and population before citing numerical claims.

## Translation into product hypotheses
| Practice | Feature | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| Systematic instruction | Demonstrate, guide, independent transfer | R1/R2 |
| Mathematical representations | Counters, diagrams, number lines | R1 |
| Story structures | Template schemas and unknown roles | R2 |
| Attention supports | Focus mode, optional breaks, narration | R3/R4 pending verification; exact UI untested |
| Child-generated models | Drawing workspace | R1; R6 pending |
| Rewards and themed play | Meaningful narrative progression | Product hypothesis |
| Adaptive rules | Targeted practice and review | Product hypothesis; thresholds unvalidated |

Research maintenance: record title, authors, date, population, intervention, comparison, outcomes, limitations and URL before promoting pending references. Do not publish unverified effect sizes. Review with an elementary educator; report usability and learning outcomes separately.
